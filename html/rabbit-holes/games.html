Pokemon Kaizo Ironmon

It's one of my favorite things to watch on Twitch. Kaizo Ironmon is an absurdly hard challenge, that usually takes about 1000 tries to complete. Some take only a minute, some hours. What makes it so hard? Everythings random. Your starter, the Pokemon you face, the items... Your opponents have their level increased by 1.5. You're not allowed to defeat wild Pokemon to level. You're not allowed to buy healing items... There are many more rules.

It's kinda like watching a kid beat up their face against a wall. Fun and fascinating.

Cassete Beasts

I ranted over mothern modern Pokemon games earlier... Cassete Beasts is a really good indie monster tamer game. Something that I wished, modern Pokemon games would be. It got heart, awesome monster designs, great soundtrack and many new fresh ideas. It doesn't use the classic double/half damage for effective attacks, it uses status effects. Water heals plants, fire makes plastic poisonous.

Persona 5

Probably the best JRPG ever made, ranked 8th on my top-100 games list. I like the core gameplay loop that levelling up your friendship makes you stronger in battles. All characters are nicely written, the story is great, the game looks fantastic, Futaba is the best waifu.
I played throught this game 3 times and spent like 150 hours with it. And it had only 2 boss fights and one dungeon-mechanic I didn't vibe with. I love this game.

Marvel Snap

I also loved this game. I played like 200 hours before the monitization finally drove me off. The biggest issue is aquiring new cards. In the beginning, you get tons cards to start with. Then they started introducing new cards. With paywalls and randomness. It was litereally impossible to get all the cards in the game - which is fine, if you can trade them for playable ones (like master duel or even hearthstone did) - and getting a competitve deck or playing with the "new toys" sometimes felt impossible. Omori

After Undertale came out, Omori was the first game that made me feel similar. It's also still one of my favorites up to this day. Maybe I can relate a bit too much to the depressive and shut-in themes in that story. I loved the characters and the creative ideas. Using emotions as status elements was such a great idea.
OMORI is one of those games that can convey emotions authentically and portray individual fates in a believable way. Yet it never loses sight of a child's imagination and joy. You're constantly driven by the desire to finally discover what's really behind the story - only to be brought back down to earth by carefree, surreal worlds. OMORI makes you happy, OMORI makes you sad; OMORI is creative and humorous. In short: In my opinion, OMORI is one of the best games that came out in the last years, and it will be hard to top it.

Gwent

CDProjectRed decided to relaunch Gwent with 3D graphics one day. The "old" game got no updates for 6 months and the new version changed the entire balance. That was when I quit. The old standalone Gwent had a really dedicated fanbase, because the game was really unique. You always played a best of three but had only one hand for the entire match. Managing ressources was an important skill. Should I lose a round on purpose, to have additional cards on the next one? Is it worth going -1 to win a round? Should I use my hero-card now or later? Can I bait a play from my opponent? It was really fun, I wish I could play that version once again... Man, sometimes I hate service games.

The Binding of Isaac

Fun fact: I started playing Isaac when the old flash version released. Good old times. I remember introducing this game to my collegues in school, when it came out and a few of them also really liked it. When rebirth dropped the game started to get an almost unlimited amout of content.
